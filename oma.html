01

How the set was built

1
Peel598 SubFam chunk consensuses read top to bottom; 26 groups called by eye, 30 chunks discarded as junk.
2
Rebuild from loci100–234 genomic copies sampled per family, aligned with SubFam’s L-INS-i parameters, 50% plurality consensus. Chunk consensuses average 50 copies each and make a family look tighter than it is.
3
Trim the edgesConservation profiled along each consensus; where it falls to the noise floor and stays, the element has ended. Fired on three families, left 23 untouched.
4
Search the genomestep1_search_extract.sh with all 26 as the query set — 90,851 merged intervals, against 77,695 from AnnoSINE’s own seeds.
5
Test the flanks80 copies per family extended 500 bp each way. A real insertion sits in unrelated context, so similarity must collapse to background on both sides.

03

What the flank test decides

Copies of a real SINE land in unrelated places, so conservation is high across the element and collapses immediately outside it — sharply, on both sides. If the flanks resemble each other, the copies are not independent insertions: a satellite, a duplicated region, or copies sitting inside one larger repeat.

The window width decides the answer. Run first with 200 bp flanks, long12 read as one-sided at 0.11/0.58; at 500 bp it is 0.12/0.10 and clean. If the window does not reach past the element, part of the measured flank is still element. Every family here uses 500 bp, and a family whose element runs to the alignment edge counts as unmeasured, not clean.

SINE2 is the one to look at by eye. It has the highest core conservation on the genome, 1.263, and its left flank still sits at 0.37 after widening. High internal coherence with a flank that will not collapse is exactly the “copies inside one larger repeat” case.
ancient78 fails on every axis. Conservation 0.169 bits with not one column reaching 1 bit, 12 hits from a genome-wide search, and both flanks as conserved as the element. Its 78 chunks are an A/T-rich collection that looked like a group only because the chunking had to put them somewhere.

05

What this does not settle

  • Coherence is not SINE-hood. The KL column says how consistently a family’s own copies agree. It says nothing about whether the element is a SINE. Several families here are highly coherent and show no resemblance to anything in RepBase or SINEBase.
  • The flank thresholds come from one genome. 0.25 and 0.40 are read off this distribution, which is bimodal enough to make the split obvious, but whether they transfer is untested.
  • The peel is one expert’s reading of one alignment, and the groups were called against the consensus set that happened to be on screen.
