Cross-tool benchmark · 119 candidate families · two species

Does the discriminator agree with tools that already know the answer?

Two independent SINE annotations were run elsewhere and published on the Tal site: 64 curated Dfam families in human, and 55 AnnoSINE_v2 de novo candidates in Timema cristinae. Neither was produced by this tool. This page scores every one of them and shows where the answers agree and where they do not.

What to look at first. The rows worth your eye are the ones this tool rejects or hedges on — use the below 50 and flagged filters. Everything scoring 100 with no flags is a clean pass and needs no review. Every set links to its alignment in the MSA viewer at both flank widths, so any verdict here can be checked by eye.

How to read a row

Each candidate is scored twice, on two different alignments of the same loci: 50 bp flanks as published, and 400 bp flanks re-extracted from the genome. The two geometries answer different questions and are not interchangeable.

50 bp gives the tighter alignment, so it is the better measure of how well copies match their consensus. 400 bp is the only width at which you can ask where does the similarity stop? — which is what separates a real insertion from a satellite, a segmental duplication, or a fragment of something longer.

decay call is the 400 bp answer to that second question:

ISOLATED_INSERTION similarity collapses to background just outside the element on both sides — the signature of a real mobile element that inserted into unrelated DNA.

ADJACENT_SIMILARITY / SATELLITE_OR_DUPLICATION / ELEMENT_CONTINUES similarity persists outward, so the copies are not in independent genomic contexts.

Human — 64 curated Dfam families

These are established, manually curated SINE families with published consensuses. There is no ambiguity about whether they are real, so this leg is a false-negative test: anything rejected here is the tool being wrong, not the annotation.

Click any column header to sort. 55 of 64 sets were re-extractable at 400 bp; the other 9 have fewer than 30 copies, which is below the minimum for a stable flank-decay measurement.

Timema — 55 AnnoSINE_v2 de novo candidates

Harder and more interesting. Timema has no pre-existing SINE library, so these candidates rest entirely on AnnoSINE_v2's structural module — TSD, poly-A, RNA-pol-III promoter boxes — with no homology backstop. They carry independent labels from the Tal analysis:

matched converged on one of the six manually curated tim/ subfamilies by reciprocal blastn (40 sets).
real unmatched, but judged genuine on inspection — substantial copy counts, near-zero assignment leak (3 sets).
noisy unmatched, with high assignment leak — fragments overlapping existing families, or structural false positives (12 sets).

The labels and this score answer different questions, and the difference matters. The matched / noisy split is about subfamily assignment — whether a candidate's copies also match some other subfamily. This tool answers only is this a SINE at all. A fragment that overlaps a real family too closely to be a distinct subfamily is still a perfectly good SINE, so a noisy candidate scoring 100 is not a disagreement. Judge this leg on the real rows and on anything scoring below 50.

53 of 55 sets were re-extractable at 400 bp. Copy counts are genome-wide totals from the Tal manifest, not the number of sequences in the alignment.
