What extension actually resolved

For most families, non-uniqueness at the original small flank was exactly a window-size artifact: extending to 250-1000 bp made it disappear, meaning the copies sit in genuinely independent places once you look far enough past the element -- the correction was right.

2 families do NOT resolve on either view, even at 1000 bp: oma_SINE5, oma_SINE9. This is the strongest evidence in the dataset for a shared, non-independent context (segmental duplication, satellite, or nested repeat) -- both the most-similar copies and a random sample keep showing shared flank sequence a full kilobase out.
4 families resolve on rand100 but not top100: oma_SINE18, oma_SINE2, oma_SINE25, oma_long12. Same pattern already interpreted for aca_SINE_0 (FINDINGS.md): only the most-similar copies share a flank, a random sample does not -- consistent with a SUBSET of copies sitting inside a shared duplicated context rather than the family as a whole.
Data-quality caveat. Several families lost a large share of their copies to the merged "(+,-)" strand defect from step1's hit-merge (documented, SINEDERELLA.md): oma_grp080 lost 14,703 of ~16,223, oma_group34 4,005 of ~4,081, oma_SINE10 1,817 of ~1,865, oma_big76 2,268 of ~2,408. oma_SINE27 lost 64 of 67 and could not be tested at all (3 usable copies, below the 12-copy floor). Their results above are real but rest on fewer, and not necessarily representative, copies.
